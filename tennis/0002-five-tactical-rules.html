Five Tactical Rules

The five highest-leverage fixes for a junior player. Each one is a habit that costs points, paired with the habit that wins them. Don't try all five at once — pick one, run it for a session, then move on. The session scorecard tracks them.

01

The Serve — chasing maximum power

Mistake

Hitting first serves at 100% effort. First-serve percentage collapses (1 in 10), and you're left with a dinked second serve that gets attacked.

Fix

Serve at 70% of maximum effort, add slice or sidespin, and aim for 6–7 out of 10 first serves in. Placement and spin beat velocity.

Why

Missing first serves hands the opponent a weak second ball and puts you under pressure immediately. At 4-all or deuce, weak second serves become double faults. Spin buys margin — even touring pros serve at roughly 70% for control.

02

Wide angles from deep behind the baseline

Mistake

Going for sharp angles or the sidelines while standing well behind the baseline.

Fix

From deep, aim higher over the net and hit deep to the middle. Save the angles for when you've stepped inside the baseline.

Why

The further back you stand, the narrower your available angles become. Sharp angles from deep produce net and wide errors. High and deep pushes the opponent back, which gives you the short ball — and then the angle is safe.

03

Recovery and split-step timing

Mistake

Standing still after hitting, recovering to the centre hash mark every time regardless of where the ball went, or still moving as the opponent makes contact.

Fix

Recover to the middle of their possible return angles — not the centre mark. Split-step as they hit.

Why

Return angles follow your shot. Hit crosscourt and recover 1–2 m onto that side; hit down the line and recover to the opposite side. Miss the position or the split-step and you can't react in time — the court is open before you've moved.

04

Aiming at the lines for winners

Mistake

Aiming directly at the singles lines when going for a winner.

Fix

Target a realistic zone inside the court. Intermediate: 1 metre inside the lines. Beginner: closer to the middle.

Why

Shots disperse — footwork and timing wobble under pressure. Aiming at the line leaves zero margin. A bigger target keeps the ball in play, and in-play balls win junior points far more often than clean winners do.

05

Going down the line too early

Mistake

Flat down-the-line shots into the open court from behind the baseline or from out wide.

Fix

Rally crosscourt and deep until you get a short ball, then step inside the baseline and go down the line.

Why

Down the line from deep has low net clearance and opens the court — the opponent counters crosscourt into the space you've vacated. Crosscourt gives more net margin and more court length, and it keeps you in position until you've earned the short ball.

How to use this

Don't chase all five. Pick the one that cost you the most points last match, make it the only thing you think about for a whole session, then score it on the session scorecard. Repeat.
